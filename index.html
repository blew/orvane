Reveal Games — the What

0. The business plan in ten lines (state on 13 Sep 2026)

  1. Product: The Proposal — a $299 hand-directed 15–20 min browser game about the couple; the last door is the question. One product. (§1, §11)
  2. Proof: Les Portes and Aurélie's reaction (with her OK) — the story is the marketing. (§7)
  3. Buyer: someone about to propose; plans 2–12 months ahead, spends ~$3k, films the moment anyway. (§13)
  4. Why not GameQ: they sell "you, in a game" in 30 minutes, kids-led; we sell the reveal and the direction, which their model can't produce. (§1)
  5. Why now: Les Portes' first playable version took 23 minutes of agent work; the cost is in the personal detail, which is what's sold. (§8)
  6. Form: asks for the relationship and the moment, not the person's stats; ~10 min, no photo, avatar cast. (§2b)
  7. Channels: story video (core) + Etsy as a service + Pinterest/YouTube/Reddit background; €50 Etsy Ads then €150 boost. (§14)
  8. Demand signal: paid orders at list price. Nothing else counts. (§11)
  9. Money: ~$284 net per order; 10 orders/month ≈ €2.6k at ~40h; season Nov–Feb doubles it. (§9)
  10. Kill rule: no paid order by ~15 Nov → stop, ≤€200 and ~60h spent. (§14)

Scope of this document: what the business is, how a customer experiences it, why it can win, and what the money looks like. No timeline, no build plan — those come only once this convinces.

Lean principle (Pascal, 13 Sep): find the right idea, the right way to expose it, and see who wants it. First orders are fulfilled by hand (Pascal + Claude); automation only once customers are actually requesting games.

Where this stands

Pascal built Les Portes for Aurélie's 42nd: a browser game where she bakes her real products while the girls cause chaos, and each recipe opens a door to a real gift she chooses. He wants a business around it.

Locked (12–13 Sep 2026): side income first (~€2–5k/month) · 10–20h/week · ≤€500 to test · free tiers by default · English only · voice-only content · Les Portes is the public demo (with Aurélie's OK) · real payments are fine, invoicing parked.

Pascal's steer (13 Sep): the customer must feel they are making the game in a semi-automated way, with the least possible human interaction — no calls, no "pure dev service". Templated games where people add their faces, words and actions. Possibly Android, not only browser.

Decided 13 Sep: avatar builder only at launch · both tiers in one funnel · Chaos Kitchen first · Android is not part of the business plan (revisit on demand) · hand-fulfilled first, automate on demand.

4. Faces, words, actions — how each is actually done

Faces. Avatar builder only at launch (decided 13 Sep); photo-stylised sprites are a later option: - Avatar builder (default, automated, instant): hair, skin, glasses, outfit, accessory → top-down sprite, Les Portes-style (no facial features, so it reads right at small sizes and there are no photos of children to store). Deterministic, free, zero privacy exposure. - Photo → stylised sprite (premium option): Cloudflare Workers AI has stable-diffusion-v1-5-img2img on the free tier Pascal already uses; docs cite "a cartoon version of a portrait" as the use case. Likeness quality is unverified — must be tested before it is promised. GameQ sells exactly this ("animated-movie version of them") at $79, so it is feasible at some quality level.

Words. The buyer's answers → an LLM drafts dialogue, hints, captions, the ending. Editable line by line. Language is a first-class field (English at launch; French/Spanish/Dutch are a prompt change, not a build).

Actions. Each template ships a menu of 8–12 events; the buyer picks and assigns. Les Portes already runs this as data (KID_EVENTS, CHAOS_ROTATION, soft-lock guards: stolen items drop after 20s, blockades clear after 90s). New events per template are the main design work.

Reveal. The DOORS / ROOMS / SCENES structure already supports "doors that open to something". Message, media, and choose-your-gift are variants of the same scene.

6. Price — what the analysis says

Anchors (corrected after the GameQ walk): GameQ ladder $79 / $159 / $279 / $399, automated, 14 verified reviews (kids) · Muksun $99 sale / $189.99 list · Songfinch $200 (human-made emotional gift that scaled to ~$75M) · NRF Valentine's spend $199.78 per person · DIY proposal extras $200–600 · Heartbit $599+ (unproven). 8. Why this can win

  1. Depth at a template price. For 15 years the choice was hand-made (Amuze Me €1–50k, dead) or generic templates (Greeting Games, ~$145k/yr). AI removes the trade-off: Les Portes' first playable version took 23 minutes of agent work; the days after were personal detail — the part customers value. Nobody at $79 offers a 20-minute story with cast, actions and a reveal.
  2. The reveal is the product. Every competitor sells "a game with your face". We sell the moment: the door that opens on the ring, the trip, the news. That's what the viral stories were about.
  3. The factory exists. Pascal already runs autonomous agent queues that build, test and deploy games (bt-chain). Order fulfilment is the same shape.
  4. The story exists. A true, filmable founder story with a working game and (with her OK) a real reaction. The three viral cases were people who spent months coding; we can say "I did this for my wife — now you can, in 20 minutes".
  5. Language is a prompt, not a build. French/Spanish/Dutch open later at near-zero cost; competitors are English-only or dead (the French ComeUp gig: 1 sale in 15 months).

10. Rejected shapes (so they stay rejected)

  • Booking a call / pure dev service: Pascal's time as the product, no leverage, and it's what BdayGame and Amuze Me did.
  • One app per customer: delivery friction killed Save the Date! and makes Muksun clunky.
  • Bespoke-only at $300+: no evidence of demand at that price yet — keep it as the upsell, not the anchor.
  • Branded marketing games for companies: saturated SaaS (BMG $0–119/month, Adact, Qualifio).
  • Physical box editions: fulfilment is what killed the Etsy shop.

12. Lean logic — the order of proof

  1. The idea — §1: a personal game with a reveal, templated, $79 / $249.
  2. The exposure — the true story plus the playable demo, put where the audience is (open: which channel first).
  3. The demand signal — real requests: paid orders, or at least completed order forms with an occasion and a date. Not likes, not views.
  4. Only then — automate the builder, add templates, consider Android.

Still open (business, not execution): which exposure to try first, what counts as "someone wants it", and the price shown at exposure.

A made-to-order proposal game delivered as a link is the same shelf, one step up.

Decision (Pascal delegated it, 13 Sep) — what runs, and in which order

Verified before deciding: Etsy's seller policy explicitly allows made-to-order digital items with "delivery of the final file … via Etsy Messages or email" (AI use must be disclosed; who_made = I did, when_made = Made to order); standalone add-ons with no item are prohibited, so the voice line is bundled, not a separate listing. Faceless voice-over accounts do grow on TikTok in 2026, but a new account's first month typically yields 500–2,000 views per video and traction takes 30–60 days — the story has to beat that baseline. New Etsy shops convert at 0.5–2%; at $299 assume the low end. TikTok Ads Manager has a ~$50/day campaign minimum, so a €150 boost runs through in-app Promote (or an Instagram boost), not Ads Manager. r/EngagementRings' rules couldn't be fetched — read them before the one story post.
